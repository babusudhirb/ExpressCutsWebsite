Men's Hair Color: Grey Coverage, Highlights and Streaks

Natural colour, grey coverage, highlights or streaks? What each one looks like, how much upkeep it takes, and what to check before any dye touches your hair.

The short answer

The main hair colour options for men are natural colour, grey coverage, highlights, streaks and bolder fashion shades. Grey coverage and natural colour are the most subtle. Highlights and streaks add visible contrast. The best choice depends on how noticeable you want the change to be and how often you will return to maintain it.

What are the main hair colour options for men?

Natural colour

A shade close to your own hair. It freshens up the colour without looking dyed, and it is the safest place to start.

Grey coverage

Colour applied to hide grey hair, either completely or in a softer way that leaves a little natural variation. Full coverage looks clean but shows at the roots as hair grows. Softer coverage tends to look more natural and is more forgiving.

Highlights

Fine sections of hair are made lighter than your base colour. Highlights add depth and movement, and they often work well on textured cuts and longer tops.

Streaks

Streaks are bolder and more defined than highlights, and they stand out on purpose. They suit people who want a noticeable style change.

Fashion colour

Silver, ash and other strong shades make a statement. They usually mean lightening the hair first and need more care to keep the colour looking fresh.

A few simple questions help narrow it down. Do you want people to notice, or just to say you look well? Will you return for touch-ups? Does your workplace have expectations? Do you want to cover grey, or do you not mind some of it showing? Your barber can also judge how a shade will sit with your skin tone and base colour.

What should you do before colouring your hair?

This part matters most. Hair dye can cause allergic reactions, and some of them are serious. The NHS advises testing the dye at least 48 hours before you use it, following the instructions in the pack, and says symptoms can take up to 72 hours to appear. NHS 111 Wales says to patch test before every use of a permanent or semi-permanent dye, even with a brand you have used before.

Before your colour appointment:

  • Tell the professional if you have ever reacted to a hair dye, or to a black henna tattoo.
  • Mention any scalp irritation, cuts or skin conditions.
  • Ask about a patch test in advance, and leave time for it before your appointment.
  • Do not use hair dye if you have had a previous reaction. Seek medical advice instead.

If you notice swelling, a rash or burning after colouring, wash the dye off and get medical advice. Treat swelling of the face or any difficulty breathing as an emergency.

How do you look after coloured hair?

  • Use a mild shampoo, and apply it to the scalp rather than scrubbing through the length.
  • Condition after each wash. Colouring can leave hair feeling drier.
  • Protect your hair from strong sun and limit very hot styling tools.
  • Plan touch-ups before the regrowth line becomes obvious.

What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Skipping the patch test. A previous good experience does not rule out a reaction.
  • Going very dark to hide grey. Flat, very dark colour can look unnatural. A softer shade is often kinder.
  • Colouring your hair but not your beard. If your beard is grey, ask whether the two should match.
  • Using box dye for a major change. Going much lighter is harder to do evenly at home.

In short

  • Natural colour and grey coverage are subtle; highlights and streaks are more visible.
  • Choose by how noticeable you want it to be and how much upkeep you will do.
  • Always follow patch-test advice. Reactions to hair dye can be serious.
  • Look after coloured hair with mild shampoo and conditioner.

Related questions

Can men cover only some of their grey?

Yes, softer coverage is possible and often looks more natural than full colour. Ask your barber what suits your hair.

How long does hair colour last on men?

It depends on the type of colour and how fast your hair grows. Permanent colour remains in the hair, but the roots will show as new hair grows out.
