How Often Should Men Get a Haircut?

There is no single schedule. Here is how your style, your hair and your standards change the answer, with typical intervals to start from.

The short answer

Most men with short hair need a haircut every three to five weeks, fades and tight tapers closer to every two to three weeks, and longer styles every six to ten weeks. These are starting points, not rules. The right interval depends on the style, how quickly your hair grows and how sharp you like to look.

Why is there no single answer?

Hair on the scalp grows at about one centimetre a month, and that is an average. What matters is how much of that growth your particular style can absorb before it loses its shape.

One centimetre barely shows on a 15 cm flow cut. On a fade where the sides were cut to one or two millimetres, the same growth means the sides are several times longer than when you left the chair. That is why two men with identical hair growth can need very different schedules.

How often should you get a haircut for each style?

Typical haircut intervals by style. These are starting points; adjust them to your own hair.
StyleTypical intervalWhat gives it away first
Skin fade or high fade2 to 3 weeksThe blend softens and the skin line disappears
Low or mid taper3 to 4 weeksThe neckline and the hair around the ears
Buzz cut or buzz fade2 to 4 weeksThe outline and the sides
Classic side part or short scissor cut4 to 5 weeksThe sides start pushing outwards
Textured cut or quiff4 to 6 weeksThe top loses shape and gets heavy
Flow cut, wolf cut or longer hair6 to 10 weeksEnds look tired and layers lose definition

What affects how often you need a trim?

Growth rate

Growth varies from person to person. If your hair always seems to look long within a fortnight, sit at the shorter end of the ranges above.

Hair texture

Curly and wavy hair hides growth better than straight hair, because the length coils rather than hangs. Straight hair shows every extra millimetre around the ears and neck.

Your job and your calendar

If you face clients, or you have a wedding or an interview coming up, you may want a tighter schedule. Book a trim two or three days before an event rather than the morning of it, so the sharpest edge has settled.

Season and comfort

Many men go shorter in hot weather and let their hair grow when it cools down. That is a personal preference, not a grooming rule.

What are the signs that a trim is due?

  • Hair has started to touch the tops of your ears.
  • Your neckline has lost its edge and looks fuzzy.
  • The sides are pushing outwards instead of lying flat.
  • You need more product, or more time, to get the same result.
  • The style that took two minutes last month now takes ten.

How do you stretch the time between haircuts?

  1. Ask for a slightly longer finish on the sides, or a lower fade. A soft taper grows out more gracefully than a skin fade.
  2. Book a neck and line tidy between full cuts, if your barber offers one. Ask at the salon what is available.
  3. Wash and style according to your hair. The American Academy of Dermatology advises using conditioner after each shampoo and matching how often you wash to how oily your hair gets.
  4. Dry your hair the way it is cut. Pushing it into place while it is damp keeps a growing style under control for longer.

Does cutting your hair make it grow faster?

Cutting happens at the ends, while growth comes from the follicle in the scalp, so a trim does not change how fast hair grows. It does keep the shape neat and removes tired ends, which can make hair look healthier and thicker while it grows.

What mistakes do people make with haircut timing?

  • Waiting until it looks bad. By then you have spent a week or two unhappy with it. Trim at the first signs instead.
  • Keeping a tight fade on a long schedule. If you will only visit every two months, a softer cut will serve you better.
  • Cutting just before an important day for the first time. Try a new style a few weeks ahead, so there is time to adjust it.

How do you find your own schedule?

Take a photo the day you get a haircut and again each week. Note when the cut stops looking right to you, and book your next visit just before that point. After two or three cycles you will know your interval, and you can read more about which style suits that routine.

In short

  • Fades: roughly every two to three weeks. Short cuts: three to five. Longer styles: six to ten.
  • Hair grows around one centimetre a month, but style decides how visible that is.
  • Softer tapers stretch the time between visits.
  • Note when your cut stops looking right and book just before that point.

Related questions

How often should I maintain a fade?

Most fades look their best for around two to three weeks. A low taper can usually go a little longer. Read our fade maintenance answer for more.

Is it bad to go a long time without a haircut?

No. Your hair will not be harmed, but short styles lose their shape and ends can start to look tired. It is mostly a question of appearance.

How long before an event should I get a haircut?

Two or three days beforehand is a good target. It gives the sharpest line time to soften and leaves room for a small adjustment if you need one.
